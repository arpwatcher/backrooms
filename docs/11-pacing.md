# 11. Pacing and anti-fatigue

Horror wears off. The tenth time you hear the same panting, it's furniture.
This chapter covers how the game keeps fear fresh over a long run, on three
time scales.

## Within a level (minutes)

A level follows a shape, not a schedule:

1. **Arrival, 0-20 s.** The lit start room is always safe. Read the place,
   find the first note (on levels 0-5), and hear the hum.
2. **Unease, roughly the first third.** Tells reach you before the thing
   does. The director keeps entities at the edge of hearing.
3. **Pressure, the middle.** Tasks, the exit search, and first sightings.
   Tension rises.
4. **Release.** Either mercy (§9), the exit, or a lit room with almond
   water.

**[built]** levers:

- Tells always come at least 5 s before contact (tested for every entity).
- The mercy rule backs entities off after sustained overload.
- Debuts happen alone, so the first time is about learning, not surviving
  two things at once.

**[next]** levers:

- **Quiet minutes.** If tension has been under 0.2 for 90 s, the director
  plays one tell from a real entity, from the direction of the exit. It is a
  nudge, not a chase.
- **The long hallway.** At least one sightline of 6+ cells on every level,
  because nothing builds dread like seeing a long way and nothing being there
  yet.

## Across a session (levels)

**[built]**:

- **Peaks** just before each checkpoint (budget x1.3).
- **Breathers** after two crowded levels (budget x0.6).
- **Silent levels**, 1 in 12: nobody lives there, but phantom tells play
  every 20-45 s. The player can't tell, and that is the point.
- **Variety**: no archetype twice in a row, and entities met in the last
  three levels are less likely.
- **Rule shifts and tasks** are each capped (60% and 55%), so a normal level
  is never rare.

## Across many runs (anti-fatigue)

What keeps the hundredth level scary:

- **Rotation of the familiar.** Famous entities get variants (Red Smiler,
  Alpha Hound) after you have survived the original 5 times **[later]**.
- **Unknowns stay unknown.** Generated entities (§10) from depth 15 mean
  there is always a rule you haven't learned **[next]**.
- **Sound decay.** Each tell sound has 3-4 variants and slight random
  detune, so the ear never fully habituates **[next]**.
- **Fake familiarity.** At low lucidity, phantom versions of known entities
  appear. You know the rule, but is this one real? **[later]**
- **Never the same sentence.** Notes are drawn from a pool and never
  repeated in one run **[built for lore notes]**.
- **Escalating notes.** Lore notes from deeper levels start referring to you
  by what you did in this run ("you ran past the party on 7") **[later]**.

## Things we never do

- No timer that kills you for being slow, outside clearly told rule shifts.
- No tell that plays only once. If you missed it, it comes again.
- No jumpscare with nothing behind it more than once per session. A fake
  scare spends trust, and trust is the budget for real ones.
- No "gotcha" deaths. A death you could not have seen coming is a bug.
